## Camelot today

{% include bunkhouse_summary.html part='what_is_camelot' %} The cabin is about 2.5 hours from Boston and currently sleeps {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.capacity_now }} people across the loft, living room, and kitchen. To visit, see the [Camelot rental page](/rentals/camelot).

Camelot’s loft had to be closed recently because it did not meet modern safety standards; it reopened only after a major repair effort by the cabin managers, and patches like this can only go so far. Making the existing cabin sleep {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.bunkhouse_capacity }} people would mean bringing it up to current code for lodging (sprinklers, fire-rated sleeping rooms, and emergency exits), and the cabin managers and the Town of Groton found no viable way to do that. Building new is the more cost-effective option.

## The bunkhouse vision
{: #vision}

After over a decade of planning, focus groups, discussions with MIT, and design work by Maclay Architects, we believe that building a new bunkhouse on the existing Camelot land is the best path forward. The new bunkhouse will be sited {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.distance_to_bunkhouse }} from the existing cabin and will provide heated sleeping space in a modern, safe, tightly insulated building enclosure.

**Who it’s for:** The bunkhouse supports MITOC’s goal of making outdoor recreation accessible, especially to new students, with inexpensive, year-round sleeping space that requires no experience or special equipment.

**Capacity:** The bunkhouse will sleep {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.bunkhouse_capacity }} people ({{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.room_count }} rooms of {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.room_size }}), enough for all but the busiest weekends of the year.

**Amenities:** The bunkhouse will have electricity, electric heat, and composting toilets.

**Fire safety:** A sprinkler system fed by a cistern in the basement, 1-hour fire-rated sleeping rooms, an emergency exit window in every room, and a networked fire alarm.

**No kitchen:** The bunkhouse will not have a kitchen. Building code requirements would have made including one prohibitively expensive. Instead, the existing cabin, expected to remain usable as a kitchen and lounge for another 10 to 20 years, will continue to be used for cooking.

For more information on the design of the bunkhouse, see [Project Details](/camelot/project).

## The plan

From 2019 through 2021, the Outing Club worked with Maclay Architects to create a bunkhouse design that complies with all modern building codes, is satisfactory to MIT Facilities and the local town board, and yet maintains the wilderness ethos of the Outing Club and its Camelot property.

## Timeline

* **2011:** Discussions begin
* **2013:** The MITOC Board of Directors (BOD) approves the project
* **2014–2018:** Focus groups about club needs and the future of Camelot
* **2018:** A generous initial donation in memory of Joris Naiman kick-starts active development
* **2019–2021:** Design with Maclay Architects
* **2022–2024:** Phase 1 fundraising ({{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.phase1_raised }})
* **2023:** The Groton planning board approves the project
* **2024–2025:** Phase 1 construction: a new bridge, off-street parking lot, and access trail, along with the final construction drawings
* **2026–2027:** Phase 2 fundraising, now under way
* **Planned for {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.groundbreaking }}:** Groundbreaking! This requires raising the full {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.total_cost }}
* **Planned for {{ site.data.bunkhouse_settings.facts.completion }}:** Construction complete!

## Costs
{: #funding}

This includes construction of the new parking lot, which was necessary for Town of Groton approval; construction of the new access trail and bridge, which are necessary for construction vehicles and fire safety; construction of the bunkhouse itself; as well as design and project support work from architects, structural engineers, geotechnical engineers, mechanical, electrical, and plumbing (MEP) engineers, and MIT Facilities.

We also made significant progress with MIT administrators and facilities to approve the construction project and plans.

## Bunkhouse team
{: #leaders}

Relaunched in Fall 2026, an eager committee of current MITOCers are spearheading fundraising and current project management! The fundraising committee includes graduate students, alumni, and affiliates who are MITOC trip leaders, board members, and lovers-of-Camelot.

David Lawrence ‘14 (former MITOC president and Camelot manager) and Greg Wallace (MITOC’s alumni liaison), who helped lead Phase 1, remain involved.

We have active participation from the MIT Department of Facilities, the MIT Division of Student Life, and the MITOC Board of Directors. To chat with us directly, reach out over email to [mitoc-bunkhouse@mit.edu](mailto:mitoc-bunkhouse@mit.edu).
